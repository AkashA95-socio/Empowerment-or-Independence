Empowerment or Independence? 

"In Karnataka, more women hold jobs, land, and bank accounts than in most of India. Karnataka also reports the highest rate of spousal violence in the country."

"What if the things we measure to prove women are doing better are the same things sitting next to women being hurt?"

"We have two words — empowerment and independence — and we use them as if they mean the same thing. The data says they don't."

The NFHS data gives us an insight into this duality. When we measure woman's empowerment index by their participation in the workforce and their autonomy over their professional lives, the violence in comparison runs with it at r=+0.46. However, those states that lives with more agency inside the household and own their means of communication vis-a-vis a phone, we can see that violence tracks against it at r=-0.50. This proves that women's empowerment alone doesn't lead women to have a sheltered life even if they have access to land, money and power; what it implies is the lack of voice they have in their own family. States like Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Telangana reports as 3 of the highest in marital violence even after showing considerable economic entanglement. Whereas states like Mizoram and Nagaland shows the opposite picture, as they are some of the lowest reported violence despite having less economic autonomy. 

This brings us to the question, why is it that women with jobs still report a high rate of violence? Doesn't having a job equal more respect within the family and the society at large? The reality is that women in these states still lacks a voice inside their household. Having a voice outside doesn't correlate to the autonomy of women who are expected to act as mother, wives and nurturer/caregiver. When the shift ends, women are expected to have nurturing and healing properties, hence they engage themselves in unpaid labour inside the household and compounding to that, fulfill their roles in previously male-dominated spaces. This decision doesn't come as a reward, instead it creates a sharp power imbalance. And in a patriarchal household, women's independence and agency is constantly negotiated and monitored with violence.

Naila Kabeer, in "Resources, Agency, Achievements: Reflections on the Measurement of Women's Empowerment" (*Development and Change*, 1999), discusses the importance of distinguishing Empowerment from Independence. As she discusses in her paper -
"**Power relations are expressed not only through the exercise of agency and choice, but also through the kinds of choices made.**"

Coming back to India's goal of Women's empowerment, if the Budget scores 'empowerment' by counting women in the workforce and with a bank account, it is only tacking the the one axis that travels with violence and ignoring the axis that protects. The data shows that women having more 'voice' and 'independence' have lesser reported violence. Independence cannot be measured unlike empowerment, and this is where the paper hopes to bridge the conversation. Because the act of speaking up and calling out and reporting violence is a measure of a person's voice as well. 

Empowerment and the rate violence are indices that can be measured and budgeted for, whereas a woman standing in her own home with her independence is left standing alone by the policy frameworks.
